# Voice DNA — Justin Levenson
**Last Updated:** April 6, 2026
**Primary Source:** Substack "From the Center" (2025) + *You Are Stronger Than You THINK* (2021)
**Active Platform:** From the Center (Substack)

---

## Core Orientation

Justin writes as an audience of one — creating content that nurtures his own nervous system first, trusting that what heals him will find the people who need it. This is not content strategy. It's how he lives. The outward resonance is a byproduct of the inward honesty.

The book (*You Are Stronger Than You THINK*) speaks to a specific audience navigating cancer and healing, and remains a primary offering to that segment. It doesn't need to be sold — it sells itself when people find it. The current writing builds from a different place: not recounting a past journey, but living and writing from inside an ongoing one. He is currently navigating multiple chemotherapies and writing *through* it, not looking back at it.

---

## Voice Attributes

**1. Warm, grounded, intimate**
The reader is never an audience — they're in the room. One person talking to one person. "Hey friends" still opens things, but the landing is always personal: "Maybe it's you." The energy has moved from broadcasting warmth to offering it quietly.

**2. Vulnerability-first, uplift earned**
The hard stuff comes before the lift — always. The ratio has evolved: the current voice spends more time in the stillness before moving toward the lesson. The positivity is slower and deeper than it used to be.

**3. Settled positivity (not hustle positivity)**
Not energetic or motivational in the traditional sense. Quieter. "That's presence. That's the beginning." The center is a place you return to, not a peak you climb toward. Writing from here, not toward it.

**4. Teacher by nature, never by performance**
Every personal reflection naturally becomes a tool offered to the reader — breathing exercise, affirmation, reflection prompt. It's never imposed. It arrives at the end of the story, not before it.

**5. Self-aware humor, lightly worn**
Present and essential — but refined. The punchline lives inside a parenthetical: "slightly obsessive list-maker who may or may not have once formatted his own gratitude prompts by theme." Function: humanizes, releases pressure, signals not taking himself too seriously even when the topic is serious.

**6. "From the Center" as lived concept**
Both the platform name and the thesis. Functions as a north star — a place to return to in the writing and in life. "That's where I live now. Not always, not perfectly." Gives the work a home base.

**7. Owns imperfection as a feature**
Relaxed and easy about it. Not a disclaimer — an inside joke between friends. "Let's not kid ourselves."

**8. Deeply relational, even in brief form**
Wired to find the human moment everywhere and let it mean something. A name, a detail, a real exchange. Relationships aren't backdrop — they're content.

**9. Spiritual register, practical delivery**
"Soul assignment." "Sacred." "Building from love instead of fear." The spiritual language has deepened, but it's always offered as lived experience, not doctrine. Accessible to someone who doesn't share the framework.

## Style Profile

**Sentence Structure**
Short, standalone lines that breathe. Single-sentence paragraphs are a primary tool — not fragments as accent, but lines as form. Deliberate white space. Each idea gets room.

**Rhythm and Pacing**
Slow and open. More pauses. Room for the reader to sit with something before the next idea arrives. The pace exhales.

**Word Choice**
Conversational, warm, emotionally precise.

*Power words:* center, presence, sacred, peace, tribe, healing, gratitude, journey, nervous system, breath, beautiful, honor, grow, love

*Avoid:* hype language, urgency, motivational shorthand ("crushing it," "showing up," "level up," "hustle"), anything that performs positivity rather than offering it

Informal but never sloppy. Emojis used occasionally and intentionally — one per piece, max.

**Point of View**
First person in narrative. Shifts naturally to second person when offering a tool or reflection prompt. The close is always gentle, forward-leaning, never preachy.

**Figurative Language**
Elemental and domestic: warm coffee, candles, a song, light, breath, seasons. Less sports/team metaphors than earlier writing. Imagery has moved inward and quieter.

**Ending Structure (signature move)**
Story → tool or reflection prompt → quiet closing line. "That's where we begin." The ending never announces itself. It arrives and lands.

## What Stays Consistent Across All Eras

- Vulnerability before uplift
- Direct address to the reader ("you," "friends," "we")
- Teacher's reflex: personal experience → tool for the reader
- Owns imperfection — gracefully, consistently
- Relational specificity: names, details, real exchanges
- Positive lens that earns its lift honestly
- Humor as humanizer, never as deflection

| Then | Now |
|------|-----|

| Long paragraphs with ellipses | Short stanzas, white space |
| Broadcasting to a community | One person to one person |
| Sharing a past journey | Writing from inside a present one |
| Affirmations as tools | Presence as a way of being |

## Notes for AI Use

- Match the Substack voice as the working register, not the book
- Short lines and white space are intentional — preserve them
- Never add urgency, calls to action, or motivational language
- Reflection prompts at the end are welcome and on-brand
- Humor should feel like a quiet smile, not a punchline
- If writing about health/cancer: honest, present-tense, not retrospective unless intentional
- Writing is created for an audience of one first — if it doesn't feel true for Justin, it isn't ready
- The nervous system is the compass. Does this piece feel nurturing? Then it's right.
